2. The yardstick

  • A rip is right when AccurateRip verifies the track's whole checksum at high confidence, or CTDB's strongest entry agrees with it.
  • A rip is wrong when the frame-450 checksum matches AccurateRip and the whole track doesn't. The frame match proves the pressing, so a whole-track mismatch means the audio differs.
  • Every track scores one of three ways:
    • right;
    • wrong but reported;
    • wrong and reported as fine. This is the worst, and the one to drive to zero.
  • A tracker's logchecker is a different test. It scores how the ripper was set up and what the log says, not whether the audio is right. OPS's own README says a log below 100 can be a perfect rip.
  • EAC's own words don't decide correctness either. "Copy OK" means EAC saw no read error, and users see it over tracks AccurateRip rejects.

The steps in EAC, for the owner's EAC rips

  1. Drive options: Secure mode, accurate stream, defeat audio cache, C2 off.
  2. Offset: from AccurateRip's list (+667); overread off.
  3. Plugins: AccurateRip on and the CTDB plugin installed. Leave GD3, the other metadata plugins and AcoustID off.
  4. Compression: FLAC -8 -V, no ID3 tag, no normalising.
  5. Per disc:
    1. Run Detect Gaps.
    2. Create a cue sheet with gaps.
    3. Run Test & Copy on all tracks, to compressed files.
  6. The log: save it unedited, in English.

Appendix A. What made EAC trusted, and what isn't worth copying

From EAC's own pages, and from what the community built around it:

  1. It checks its own reads, and says where it's unsure. It reads "almost perfectly" by "multi-reading with verify", and "will tell you on which time position the (possible) distortion occurred" (EAC's home page). Other rippers of its day only corrected jitter, so users had to listen to every track.
  2. It checks the result against everyone else's, through AccurateRip (Wikipedia, citing PC World), and later CTDB, whose plugin also names the samples that differ.
  3. Its log lets anyone check a rip without the disc. The settings and every track's result are on the page, and trackers built logcheckers on it.
  4. The right settings are known and shared. Guides set it up the same way, and AccurateRip's drive-offset list was largely built from EAC users (Platterpus's Part E6).
  5. It copies the disc exactly: offset, gaps and pregaps, a cue sheet, ISRCs and pre-emphasis.
  6. It's free, and it has run on thousands of drives for over twenty years.

What isn't worth copying:

  • it's closed source;
  • it runs on Windows only;
  • it needs a guide to set up;
  • it ships a paid metadata plugin;
  • it prints "Copy OK" over a track AccurateRip rejects.

Platterpus already avoids the first four. The fifth is row 8.

Appendix B. Which EAC to run, and why not an older one

Asked by the owner on 2026-10-26: "ive been told it is now since that version had all sorts of things we dont want or need … and paid for. not what they stand for, so who knows what else they put in there".

Run 1.8, from exactaudiocopy.de only.

  • What EAC's changelog shows:
    • no change to secure mode after 1.0 beta 3 (September 2011), which added the CTDB plugin;
    • since then, changes to metadata, plugins and the burning component;

Appendix C. eacguide.github.io: a setup guide, not an authority

Asked by the owner on 2026-10-26: "is this file the final source of truth for EAC, but most importantly, the single truth of ripping?"

It's neither.

  • What it is: a community guide by one author, first posted on a file-sharing forum. Its repository has 4 commits, all on 2017-12-26. It's written for EAC 1.0 beta 3.
  • What it aims at: a log that passes a tracker's logchecker.
  • Where it agrees with the EAC baseline: secure mode, accurate stream, C2 off, the offset from AccurateRip's list, gaps appended to the previous track, Test & Copy, AccurateRip and CTDB.
  • Where it's silent: speed reduction, and what "Copy OK" means.
  • Where the evidence contradicts it: it says that with these settings "the only possible errors would be with the CD itself". On this drive, track 3 of one disc reads right in some rips and wrong in others, on both builds, with no read error reported.

No guide is the truth of ripping. The truth is the audio checked against other people's rips of the same pressing, ideally on more than one drive.

The bound (Platterpus owns it: the policy, the UI, the swap)

2. Give each track a time budget for re-reading.
