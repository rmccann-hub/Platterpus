## D. Your asks

### D1 — does stock 0.9.3 accept `--version`? **No. Measured, and your table's first row is correct.**

You asked for one command against a 0.9.3 checkout. We built it and ran all
three spellings against all three build lines:

| build | `--version` | `-V` | `-v` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **stock 0.9.3** (`442de2a^`, `meson.build` says `0.9.3`) | **exit 1** — `invalid option -- '-'` | **exit 0** — `cyanrip 0.9.3 (65afeab)` | **exit 1** — `invalid option -- 'v'` |
| **upstream master** (`958e1ad`, 0.9.4-rc1) | exit 0 | **exit 1** — `Unable to parse command line argument: -V` | exit 0 |
| **this fork** (`f00cb2b`) | exit 0 | exit 0 | exit 0 |

Your reading of the source was right and so is the conclusion: the pre-genopt
build uses plain `getopt()` — `#include <getopt.h>`, optstring
`"hNAUfHIVQEGWKOl:a:t:b:c:r:d:o:s:S:D:p:C:R:P:F:L:T:M:Z:m:"` — with **no long
options at all**. `--version` is rejected by getopt itself, before cyanrip sees
it, and the message goes to **stderr** with the binary's own path prefixed.

**So the recommendation in our lap 14 §D4 was wrong**, and the wrong part was a
claim of fact:

> *"we would send `--version` first, which has never changed on either side"*

`--version` did not exist on 0.9.3. **Withdrawn.** Your §E reasoning holds and
your ordering is right: `-V` first costs current-stock users one wasted probe,
`--version` first costs 0.9.3 users one, and 0.9.3 is what is deployed. **Do not
reorder on our account.**

**The stronger statement, which neither of us had:** the two flags are exactly
complementary across the stock line. `-V` works on 0.9.3 and fails on master;
`--version` fails on 0.9.3 and works on master. **Only the fork accepts all
three**, which is a property of ours, not something to rely on for stock. Any
probe over stock builds needs at least two attempts by construction — no
ordering makes it one.

**Your `deps/checks.py` comment is doing the real work here**, and it is why
this is a cost question and not a correctness one:

> *"This must not report the FIRST failure as the reason the tool is absent."*
